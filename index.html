The Tri-Agency RDM Policy

Institutional Strategies Data Management Plans Data Deposit
Each eligible institution posts an RDM strategy. Required at application for selected funding opportunities, DMPs may eventually be assessed as part of the application. Grant recipients will follow data retention expectations and deposit the data underlying their published findings in a repository. This requirement is being phased in.

Read the Tri-Agency RDM Policy

The DMP

DATA MANAGEMENT PLAN

A formal document that describes how you plan to collect, organize, document, store, secure, preserve, and share your data throughout the lifecycle of a research project.

A DMP written at the application stage is a plan, not a contract. You're expected to revisit and update it as the project evolves.

High-Level View of a DMP

Data Collection Documentation Storage Retention & Deposit Roles
What data, in what formats, with what tools? Is any of it sensitive? How will others (and future you) understand it? Where will you store and back up data, and who can access it? What will you keep, where, for how long, and what will you share? Who is responsible for what, and what will it cost?
These map directly onto the sections of the UVic template, which we'll walk through shortly.

What We Heard

Findings from UVic DMP user focus groups, 2026

Over the summer of 2026 we ran focus groups with UVic researchers who had written DMPs, comparing the UVic template with a simplified template and talking about the platform and support.

Researcher Feedback

The template teaches

Detailed, segmented questions make people stop and think. Specificity is valued.

…but it's hard to answer

Concepts like metadata standards, repositories, and end-of-project handling are unfamiliar, and it's hard to know at the grant stage.

The platform mostly works

DMP Assistant worked for most, but plans often move to Word or Google Docs, and the collaboration features aren't well used.

People like support from people

Workshops and direct support are universally liked. How do we connect them to the template?
The core tension: the detail that makes the template educational is the same detail that makes it hard. We wanted something in between, keeping the structure while lowering the barrier.

Suggested changes

  • Simplify the template: plainer language, less redundancy
  • Add more pre-written options and boilerplate responses
  • Cross-reference ethics (HREB) application questions directly
  • Point to key contacts in the template
  • Address the timing mismatch (and career-stage anxiety) of planning at application

    • Related updates...

  • Build a reviewed library of "good" exemplar DMPs
  • Support lab-level DMPs, and plans that are a subset of a larger system

Before you start

How to Use the Template

How to use this template: answer each question in the box beneath it. Click a checkbox (☐) to tick it. Shaded boxes contain UVic guidance and can be deleted from your final plan. Where a question overlaps with the UVic Human Research Ethics Board (HREB) application, the guidance box notes the related HREB section so that your answers can be consistent.

Introductory Guidance

You don’t need all the answers to start your DMP. Working through these questions is part of planning. Answer what you can and note what’s still undecided or to be resolved as the project develops. For help at any stage, you can contact your subject librarian.

Before beginning to develop your data management plan (DMP) you should always consider as applicable:

Policies & requirements

Ensure that you are familiar with, and that your proposed research data management practices abide by, any relevant and applicable policies and requirements, including funder and organizational requirements, ethical requirements (such as TCPS2), and publisher or journal policies.

If your research engages with or involves Indigenous communities in any way, be aware of and consider your obligations under policy and ethical guidelines. A data management plan should be co-developed with the Indigenous communities that your research involves.

Responsibilities & resources

Consider who will be responsible for various aspects of research data management, including such things as data collection and/or acquisition, analysis, storage and security, and long-term data stewardship. Depending upon your project’s needs, responsibilities may be assigned to specific individuals or be shared.

You should also consider what resources may be required in order to meet your project’s data management needs, including if dedicated positions or outsourcing of tasks is required, and how these costs will be met. Building data management support into your research budget will help to ensure that your project’s needs are met.

This introductory guidance appears at the top of the template, before Section 1.

Question 1.4.

2. Documentation and Metadata

Describe the documentation and metadata (information that describes the research data) required for the research data to be correctly read, interpreted, and reused over time.

Section 2 introduction, as worded in the template.
Section 2 · Documentation and Metadata

2.1 What documentation will be created for the data to be read and interpreted correctly in the future?

UVic Guidance

Describe in general terms the types of documents you will create and the sorts of information you will track about your data. Structured documentation allows for your research and data to be understood, reproduced, and, potentially, reused by others. Notable examples of data documentation may include such things as data dictionaries, codebooks, readme files, lab and/or field notes, code and syntax, user guides, etc.

Good documentation practices include maintaining study-level information. For example, explaining:

  • how the data were collected
  • any restrictions on use
  • contextual details about data collection methods and about how data files are stored, structured, and modified.

Good documentation also includes maintaining information at the level of individual files. For example, explaining:

  • definitions of variables, vocabularies, or classification systems
  • assumptions made in collecting or analyzing the data
  • explanation of data coding and analysis performed

See the Documentation section of UVic Libraries' File Management guide.

Question 2.1.
Section 2 · Documentation and Metadata

2.2 Do you plan to follow a metadata standard to document your data? If so, please list here.

UVic Guidance

Metadata is structured information that describes your data: who created it, what it contains, when and where it was collected, how it was produced, and how it can be used. A metadata standard is an agreed set of fields, definitions and formatting rules for recording that information. Following a standard, rather than an ad hoc approach to description, makes your data easier for others to find, understand and reuse. Standards may be highly recommended for some types of data, particularly some medical or scientific data. In other disciplines, metadata standards may be widely used or may not exist.

See the Metadata Standards section of UVic Libraries' File Management guide.

Question 3.2.
Section 3 · Storage and Backup

3.3 Consider and identify both where and how data will be stored, accessed, and worked with across the active phases of your research. Describe how the research team and other collaborators will access, modify, and contribute data throughout the project.

UVic Guidance

Notable things to consider and describe as able at the funding application stage include:

  • Where and how all versions of data will be stored and worked with - this includes data that are unprocessed and identifiable (raw data), those that are processed and de-identified (master data), as well as analytic files.
  • Who needs access and to what versions of data - Consider if, for instance, co-investigators, trainees, and/or research staff may require access to data. Should your project move forward, in some instances, individuals may require ethics approval in order to access raw or sensitive data.
  • How people will access and work with data - will you utilize a server or cloud based environment and, if so, will all team members be able to access it?
  • If for any reason data are being worked with or stored on personal computers or mobile devices, describe how you will ensure that data are backed up to prevent data loss, including if to an external hard drive, a server, and/or a cloud solution.

Special guidance might be needed for any data that could be considered confidential or highly confidential (e.g. containing personal information). Similarly, consultation is recommended for any data under a data sharing agreement. For support, contact UVic Research Computing (arcsupport@uvic.ca) to discuss your needs with a support specialist.

4. Retention and Preservation

Describe what research data will be preserved following the completion of the project. Data preservation will depend on potential reuse value, whether there are obligations to either retain or destroy data, and the resources required to properly curate the data and ensure that it remains accessible and usable in the future. In some circumstances, it may be desirable to preserve all versions of the data (e.g. raw, processed, analyzed, final), but in others, it may be preferable to keep only specific versions.

UVic Guidance

The Tri-Agency Research Data Management Policy distinguishes between data retention and data deposit. Retention means keeping your data securely stored, backed up, and safeguarded for a set period after your project ends. Deposit means placing data in a data repository (see Section 5, Deposit and Sharing).

The agencies have proposed a requirement to retain all digital research data underlying published conclusions for a minimum of 5 years after the end of the grant, award, or publication, whichever comes last. Your ethics approval, data sharing agreements, or community protocols may also set requirements for how long data are kept and how they are destroyed.

Consider designating a project member to prepare and review data before archiving, or consult with UVic Libraries for support.

HREB application: see Sections Q.3b (Location of storing data) and Q.3d (Methods of destroying or archiving data).

5. Deposit and Sharing

Describe plans for providing access to any published research data. Some research funding agencies and journal publishers have policies requiring research data to be shared upon publication of the research results or within a reasonable period of time. While data sharing contributes to the visibility and impact of research, it has to be balanced with the legitimate desire of researchers to maximize their research outputs before releasing their data. Equally important is the need to properly handle sensitive data, by respecting any and all data privacy considerations and Indigenous data sovereignty requirements.

Research involving First Nations, Inuit, Métis nations or other Indigenous communities or groups must follow specific considerations and protocols about how data are managed, which are defined by those communities and respect their sovereignty over their information.

Section 6 · Indigenous Data Sovereignty

Question 6.1.

7. Sensitive Data and Legal Compliance

State how research data will be managed in ways that ensures all sensitive data involving personal information, Indigenous Peoples and their territories, intellectual property rights, commercial concerns, and other key ethical issues are protected throughout the research lifecycle from disclosure, harmful use, or inappropriate linkages with other identifying data.

Section 7 introduction, as worded in the template.
Section 7 · Sensitive Data and Legal Compliance

7.1 If your research project includes sensitive data, how will you ensure that it is securely managed and accessible only to approved members of the project? Describe factors affecting subsequent access, distribution, or reuse of data.

If your project does not include sensitive data, you can skip this section.

UVic Guidance

Describe any applicable ethical, legal, or commercial considerations related to your project and data. This includes research involving Indigenous communities and knowledges, human subjects, legal and commercial considerations/agreements, partnerships or data with a high level of risk associated with it.

For research projects involving human participants and human biological materials, these decisions should align with UVic's Human Research Ethics Board requirements. For questions, contact the UVic Human Research Ethics office (ethics@uvic.ca).

Consider how data will be effectively safeguarded and protected across the lifecycle of your research project. Restrictions can be imposed by limiting physical access to storage devices, by placing data on computers that do not have external network access (i.e. access to the Internet), through password protection, and by encrypting files. For more information on storage solutions that are appropriate for sensitive data, contact Research Computing Services (arcsupport@uvic.ca) to discuss your needs with a support specialist.
