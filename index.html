Welcome. Press 's' to open speaker notes. Today: a quick refresher on why DMPs matter for Tri-Agency funding, then most of our time on the changes we've made to the UVic DMP Template in response to what researchers told us in focus groups.

What is Research Data Management?

The processes of managing data throughout the research lifecycle to guide the collection, documentation, storage, sharing, and preservation of research data.

Good RDM benefits the community and the original researcher.

RDM isn't a one-time task at the end of a project. It runs alongside the whole research lifecycle, from planning and collection through to preservation and reuse.

What are Research Data?

Data that are used as primary sources to support technical or scientific enquiry, research, scholarship, or creative practice, and that are used as evidence in the research process and/or are commonly accepted in the research community as necessary to validate research findings and results.

Tri-Agency Research Data Management Policy (2021), definitions.

Research data spans disciplines: surveys, interviews, images, code, field notes, instrument output, archival sources, 3D models, and more.

Emphasise that this applies to every discipline, not just the sciences. Humanities and creative-practice researchers have research data too.

The Tri-Agency RDM Policy

CIHR, NSERC and SSHRC, building on the Tri-Agency Statement of Principles on Digital Data Management (2016)

Institutional Strategies Data Management Plans Data Deposit
Each eligible institution posts an RDM strategy, including UVic. Required at application for selected funding opportunities, with more added over time. DMPs may be assessed as part of the application. Grant recipients will deposit the data, metadata and code that support their research conclusions in a digital repository. This requirement is being phased in.

Read the Tri-Agency RDM Policy

The three pillars of the policy. Check the agencies' page for the current list of funding opportunities that require a DMP. It changes as new opportunities are added. Even when a DMP isn't required, writing one is good practice and is expected under the policy's principles.

The Solution

DATA MANAGEMENT PLAN

A formal document that describes how you plan to collect, organize, document, store, secure, preserve, and share your data throughout the lifecycle of a research project.

It is a living document!

A DMP written at the application stage is a plan, not a contract. You're expected to revisit and update it as the project evolves.

High-Level View of a DMP

Data Collection Documentation Storage Retention & Deposit Roles
What data, in what formats, with what tools? Is any of it sensitive? How will others (and future you) understand it? Where will you store and back up data, and who can access it? What will you keep, where, for how long, and what will you share? Who is responsible for what, and what will it cost?

After Steeves & Wolf, nyu-dataservices.github.io/WritingDMP-GrantApplications

These map directly onto the sections of the UVic template, which we'll walk through shortly.

How It Works: DMP Assistant

A free, bilingual, national DMP tool from the Digital Research Alliance of Canada.

  1. Go to dmp-pgd.ca and sign in, choosing University of Victoria as your organization
  2. Create a plan and select the University of Victoria DMP Template (UVIC)
  3. Answer the questions with UVic guidance alongside
  4. Share with collaborators, or export to Word/PDF for your application

Also in DMP Assistant

  • Discipline-specific templates

What We Heard

Findings from UVic DMP user focus groups, 2026

Over the summer of 2026 we ran focus groups with UVic researchers who had written DMPs, comparing the UVic template with a simplified template and talking about the platform and support.

Focus Group Findings

The template teaches

Detailed, segmented questions make people stop and think. Specificity is valued.

…but it's hard to answer

Concepts like metadata standards, repositories, and end-of-project handling are unfamiliar, and it's hard to know at the grant stage.

The platform mostly works

DMP Assistant worked for most, but plans often move to Word or Google Docs, and the collaboration features aren't well used.

People like people

Workshops and direct support are universally liked. How do we connect them to the template?
The core tension: the detail that makes the template educational is the same detail that makes it hard. We wanted something in between, keeping the structure while lowering the barrier.

What Researchers Suggested

  • Simplify the template: plainer language, less redundancy
  • Add more pre-written options and boilerplate responses
  • Cross-reference ethics (HREB) application questions directly
  • Point to key contacts in the template
  • Address the timing mismatch (and career-stage anxiety) of planning at application
  • Build a reviewed library of "good" exemplar DMPs
  • Support lab-level DMPs, and plans that are a subset of a larger system
The first five shaped the template revision you'll see next. Exemplars and lab-level plans are on our roadmap for 2027.

Change 1

Less Redundancy

"Simplify the template: language, redundancy."

Removed or mergedWhat happened
Describe how you will organize, name, and version-control your filesRemoved; file organization now sits in the Section 1 introduction
How will you make sure documentation is captured consistently?Removed; 2.1 asks what documentation you will create
Where will hardcopy notebooks and physical samples be stored?New Physical storage checkbox in 3.2
How will the team access, modify, and contribute data?Merged into 3.3 storage & access across active phases
How will you ensure your data is preservation friendly?Removed; Section 4 guidance covers de-identification, software and metadata needed for reuse
Each removed question was either covered by another question or by the guidance. Fewer, clearer questions, without losing the coverage.
Change 2

Explaining Unfamiliar Concepts

"Metadata standards, repositories, end-of-project handling remain unfamiliar."

Metadata standards

A plain-language definition: what metadata is, what a standard is, and why following one helps reuse.

Retention vs. deposit

Explains the Tri-Agency distinction: retention = keeping data safe after the project; deposit = placing it in a repository.

Licences

New question on terms of reuse, with each Creative Commons option explained in one line.
The retention guidance also notes the agencies' proposed minimum retention of 5 years after the end of the grant, award or publication (whichever is last) for data underlying published conclusions. Ethics approvals, agreements and community protocols may also set retention and destruction requirements.

Change 5

Who to Ask, Right Where You Need It

"Refer to key contacts in the template."

If you're stuck on…The template now points you to

This responds to the finding that workshops and direct support are universally liked. The template now hands people off to a real person at the moment they need one.
Change 6

Built to Travel Beyond the Platform

"At some stage it moves to a Google or Word doc."

A Word version of the template

  • Cover table: title, PI, contributors, abstract, funder, grant ID, date, version
  • Clickable checkboxes
  • Numbered questions that match DMP Assistant

Easy to finalize

  • "How to use this template" instructions up front
  • Guidance in shaded boxes you can delete from your final plan
  • Introductory guidance on policies, responsibilities, and resources
Rather than fight the move to Word, we support it. Start in DMP Assistant or in Word; the numbering matches either way. The version field supports the "living document" message.
